# LoomRoom

The game is called **The Table Awaits** (the product name since 2026-10-22; The Table Waits from 2026-10-13, LoomRoom before); the project, repository and code keep the name LoomRoom. First-person 3D RPG. Unity 6000.5, URP, Cinemachine 3, Input System, Odin Inspector, Pixel Crushers Dialogue System, Synty art. Two controllable players: the **Room player** (exploration, never fights) and the **Table player** (first-person arms, melee combat). Rewritten in September 2026 on the conventions used in LoomyDungeons.

## Tooling

- **Use the Unity CLI (`unity-cli` skill) for all editor, scene, prefab and asset work. Never use Unity MCP servers (coplay-mcp, UnityMCP), not even to check editor state.**
- **Meshy (AI models): `python Tools/meshy.py`** (text, image, retexture, remesh, rig, concept, fetch, balance; `--help` lists options). Results go to `Assets/Game/Meshy/<name>/` (FBX, GLB, textures, thumbnail, `meshy.json` with task ids for follow-ups); finished prefabs go in `Assets/Game/Meshy/Prefabs/` (for now), mesh, texture and material in `Prefabs/<Name>/`. Pixel-art props: `Tools/pixel_bake.py` (Blender: new UVs at wall density, bake) then a painter like `Tools/barrel_texture.py`; run Pixel Look > Build Materials after. The key is in `~/.meshy/api_key`, never in the repo. There is no Meshy MCP server.

- **Synty Pixel look (no Meshy)**: every Synty item, prop and room object is restyled like the rat: `Development/Editor/PixelRestyle.cs` (+ `.Apply.cs`) gives the model fresh UVs and paints a small point-filtered texture texel by texel: Synty's colour at that spot (so labels and sigils survive), baked ambient occlusion and dirt in creases, and material grain from 3D noise (wood, metal with rust, stone, bone, cloth, gold; pale modern surfaces in the room count as paint, no rust). Emissive parts keep a separate emission map. Results live in `Assets/Game/Art/Synty Pixel/{Weapons,Items,Props,Room}/<mesh>/` (mesh, texture, material); item and prop prefabs point at them, room objects carry them as prefab overrides in `Room.unity`; Synty assets are never edited (an item using a Synty prefab directly gets a copy in `Items/Prefabs`, like `Consumables/Potion`). Characters too (skinned meshes keep their bone weights): the DM body and every DM face, enemies and NPCs (on their base prefabs), the table player's arms (`TablePlayer.prefab`), the room player (two bodies on one PolygonTown skeleton, picked by `BodyVisuals > RoomBodyType.body`: BodyA the woman, `Character_Daughter_01`, BodyB the man, `Character_Father_02`; each body's Synty head is cut away and the hole on the neck capped in skin, the neck slimmed (`<mesh> No Face.asset`; the woman's Synty hair below the head is flattened onto her back in her shirt's colour, not cut, so no hole is left), and a Meshy head sits under the head bone, sized by proportions (chin to crown = standing height / 6.4; the chin 0.24 head heights below the head bone, so chin to collarbone is about 0.4 head heights: smaller heads and a higher chin looked small on a long neck): `Meshy/RoomHeadWoman` (face 8 of the first ten concepts, hair cut to end just below the jaw, clear of the shoulders; she keeps the bust's own neck, skinned to neck_01 and head by `Tools/room_head_skin.eval.cs`, its base flared into the shirt's opening, and her body's Synty neck is cut away by `Tools/room_body_fix.eval.cs`) and `Meshy/RoomHeadMan` (the DM's Tired head), each cut at the chin, welded (Meshy heads are loose triangles; unwelded they crack), closed underneath, decimated and baked in Blender onto fresh UVs from the full-detail head (`Room Head <X>.fbx`, `Room Head <X> baked.png`), baked in Blender with short rays (`Tools/room_head_bake.py`; longer rays went through the hair strands and baked the cheek onto them), then made the way the Dungeon Master's faces are (2026-10-24): hers cut to about 2,600 triangles in Blender (`Tools/room_head_lowpoly.eval.cs` + `Tools/room_head_decimate.py`, weights from the nearest old vertex), both restyled by `PixelRestyle.Build` from Meshy's own painting at the DM faces' 40 texels a unit (`Tools/room_head_restyle.eval.cs`; `<name> restyled.*`; the faces are about four times finer than the bodies, as the DM's are, the user's choice), the skin mapped to the body's hands (`Tools/room_head_skin_tone.py`, from `<name> restyled raw.png`: median to the hands', half their spread, her forehead's tan patches filled from skin) and his eyes cleaned by place on the head (`Tools/room_head_eyes.eval.cs`: white, iris and pupil redrawn inside his own eye openings); the older painted pipeline (`room_head_views`, `room_head_paint`, `room_head_project`) is retired; the heads cull back faces and sample light probes at the body's pelvis; The bodies keep their restyle textures; the room body's `GazeLookAt` is on the new head bone; the old hunter body is kept switched off as `RoomBody (hunter)`), the dinner family and the class miniatures (cast pewter; `ClassFigures` no longer overrides their material). Skinned restyles live in `Characters/<mesh> (<source file>)/` because Synty reuses mesh names across files. The apartment blockout (`PixelRestyle.Surfaces.cs`) gets one 64 px tiling texture per flat-colour material (plaster, wood planks, linen, metal, stone) and box-projected UVs per shape and scale (`Art/Synty Pixel/Apartment/Meshes`); the room is built ~22 units to the metre, so `SurfaceTexelsPerMetre` is per unit (1.8). Textured materials (floorboards, paintings) get small point-filtered copies in `Surfaces/`. Glass, water, glows, text and the DM's eyes stay as they are. Review every rebuild against the Synty original before swapping it in. Textures are sized exactly from the unwrapped UV area (no power-of-two rounding), so every model gets the same pixel size. Restyled meshes are saved readable (`PixelRestyle.MarkReadable`): prop mesh colliders use them, and the NavMesh built with each floor can only read readable meshes in a build (never point a collider at a Synty mesh).
- **Dirty pixel art (main art pipeline)**: the `loomroom-art` skill (`.claude/skills/loomroom-art/SKILL.md`) is the main art skill; the house style is in `Docs/Pixel-texture-style.md` (cave bat and urn crawler concepts). Creatures: Meshy concept in the house style, approval, then model. Weapons and shields are painted restyles (`Development/Editor/PixelRestyle.Painted.cs`: front and back reference renders repainted with `meshy.py concept`, projected back onto fresh UVs at `PixelRestyle.ItemTexelsPerUnit`, 128 texels per unit for all new art since 2026-10-12; older art is at 150 until re-baked), in `Art/Synty Pixel/{Weapons,Shields}/<mesh>/<Item> painted.*`; every weapon and shield prefab points at one. The first try (a noise-based "dirty" grade, `<mesh> dirty.*` files) is retired; `PixelRestyle.Items.cs` now only holds `ItemTexelsPerUnit`. The Bronze Sword is `SM_Wep_Sword_03`.
## Working rule

- **Folder layout** is in `Docs/Folder-layout.md` (reorganised 2026-10-23): ours in `Assets/Game/<Area>`, bought libraries outside it (`Synty`, `Spells Pack`, `ThirdParty`), everything more than one level uses in `Levels/Shared`, script folders split by topic. Move assets only in the editor and update every written path after.

- **No obsolete APIs.** Unity 6.5: write the current call, never one marked `[Obsolete]` (CS0618/CS0612 warnings). `FindObjectsByType<T>()` / `FindObjectsByType<T>(FindObjectsInactive.Include)` with no `FindObjectsSortMode` (the sort mode is obsolete), `FindAnyObjectByType`/`FindFirstObjectByType` not `FindObjectOfType`, `GetEntityId` not `GetInstanceID`. A warning like that in a file you touch is fixed in the same change.
- **No menu entries.** Tools > LoomRoom holds only Asset Studio, the one the user asked for (F11 fullscreen is a shortcut). Never add a `[MenuItem]` for builders, setup or test code: they are static methods run from an eval. Where this file names a "Tools > LoomRoom > ..." entry, that entry is gone; run the same method from an eval.

- **Texel size is measured, never judged by eye.** Everything placed or built must match its neighbours' pixel size: the room 1.8 texels a unit, the bedroom's painted surfaces and furniture (rug, floor, the table's edge, lamps, books) and the campaign map lying among them 6.4 (`CampaignMapBuilder.MapTexelsPerUnit`, measured), the dungeon world (walls, props, items, creatures) 128 a unit. Before reporting art done, run `CampaignMapBuilder.CheckTexels("<prefab>", <texels a unit>)` (lists every textured renderer more than 20% off) and fix every line. No raw Synty atlases in the game: a Synty model always gets a pixel copy at its density (`PixelRestyle.Build`, measured and re-baked until within 5%, as `CampaignMapBuilder.PixelProps` does).
- **Texel check before showing art, every time** (the user has had to ask for it again and again). Run `TexelCheck.Scene("<scene object>", <texels a unit>)` (`Development/Editor/TexelCheck.cs`, every submesh measured by its UV area; the bedroom is `TexelCheck.Scene()`, 6.4) and fix every line it prints: work is not done until it says 0 off. Never put a texture made for another density on a surface without fitting it by measurement (the apartment's 64 px textures are 1.8, the dungeon's Aged brass 128): the bedroom's fitted copies live in `World/Art/Bedroom/Texel fit`. Round things are modelled round in Blender (candles, cups, pans, arms), never boxes. Never write a zero normal: it shades to NaN, which shows as white bloom balls in the Scene view (the game camera's Stop NaN hides them); `RoomTiles` falls back to the source normals.
- **Check the approach against the art guide before starting** (user, 2026-10-24). Open `Docs/Pixel-texture-style.md` and the `loomroom-art` skill and test the plan against each point: dirty, colour varying texel to texel, no flat fills, no airbrushed gradients, no clean cel shading, dark creases and lit edges, muted base. Characters and props are graded by the restyle (Synty's flat swatch per texel, occlusion, crease dirt, material grain; `PixelRestyle.GradeTexel` for textures made outside Build); a painted or AI texture gets that same grade, never left smooth and never cut into flat tones. A one-off look the user asked for in one place (the campaign map's flat drawn pass) is not the house style. When something has to match, match the guide first and its neighbours second, and name the guide points it meets in the report.
- **Characters are reviewed feature by feature, never as a whole** (user, 2026-10-24, after I missed eyes, brows, ears, the neck seam and gaps behind the ears). Run `Tools/character_review.eval.cs` (an eval: one sheet per character of zoomed crops, front, sides and back, in neutral light and in the room's light) and write one line per crop before reporting: eyes (white, iris and pupil each readable, nothing smeared into the white or the skin), brows (shape and taper, colour of the hair, no specks), nose and mouth, ears (an ear shape with an inner shadow, nothing cutting through it), hairline and nape (hair meets scalp, no bare patches, no gaps to the background), the neck join (no seam, no colour or grain step), the back of the head. Compare with the approved concept side by side. A crop with any fault is not done; only the user decides a visible flaw is acceptable. Checking only the faults the user named is not a review.
- **Never say done while a fault shows** (user, 2026-10-24, after I reported faces I could see were broken). Review renders use the game's own lighting with no added fill light, from where the player sees it and as close as they can get, zoomed until texels show. Write down every fault in the render (blotches, colour mismatch between parts, leaks across edges, jagged edges, wrong texel size, odd spots) and fix each one, then render again. If a fault is left, the work is not done: say so plainly and say what is still wrong, never call it finished.
- **A prop's light is the prop's child** (user, 2026-10-14 and again 2026-10-25). Every light that comes from a prop (lamp, lantern, pendant, candle, sconce, window) sits under that prop, so moving the prop moves its light; a prop I make or swap in takes over the old fixture's lights and rod or chain as children, and a tool that rebuilds it hands the lights back first. Only fills from no fixture stay loose. LightingManager's sources point at the Light components, so reparenting keeps them.
- **Look before saying done.** Any visible change (art, levels, UI, lighting, props) is captured in game, the changed part cropped and enlarged, and looked at before it is reported as done: gaps, walkability, sizes, texel size against its neighbours, how it reads from where the player stands. Passing numbers or test scripts are not a look. Report what still looks wrong.

## Settled decisions

- **The apartment is never hidden during dungeon runs.** The room is seen from the dungeon (skylights, the boss stage in front of the DM, spells that hit him), so it stays drawn even though it costs draw calls (decided 2026-10-18).
- **Our own design.** Other games were studied for feel and are inspiration only: never name them in code, assets, docs, commit messages or the game, and make our version of anything taken from them our own, not a copy.
- **Single `Assembly-CSharp`, no namespaces.** Same rule as LoomyDungeons. Do not propose asmdefs or namespaces.
- **One data asset per thing.** `CharacterData` holds what every character shares (identity, faction, stats, voice, animation triggers) and is used as-is by NPCs; `PlayerData : CharacterData` adds movement, stamina and starting items; `EnemyData : CharacterData` adds behaviour and attacks. Shared systems (health, damage, death) take `CharacterData`; player code reads `Player.PlayerData`, enemy AI reads `EnemyBrain.Data`. `ItemData` per item. A level's run is a list of `DungeonBiome` stages (look, enemies, loot profile, guardian); everyday loot is a `DungeonLootProfile` that picks catalog items by `ItemData.tier`, and hand-listed `DungeonLootTable`s are for special cases. Both are `LootSource`. `EnemyBehaviourProfile` only when several enemies must share tuning; otherwise behaviour lives on the enemy's own data. Everything lives under `Assets/Game/<Area>/`: all characters under `Characters/`, items in `Items/Data/{Weapons,Armor,Shields,Consumables,Trinkets}` (retired ones in `_Archive`), spells in `Combat/Spells/<Spell>`, custom effect assets in `Items/Effects`, audio data in `Audio/Data`, level assets in `Levels/`.
- **A character is one prefab file.** Its data (`PlayerData`, `EnemyData` or `CharacterData`) is stored inside the prefab as a sub-asset and edited on its Character component. Enemies are variants of `Characters/Enemies/Base/{Enemy base, Humanoid enemy base}`, NPCs of `Characters/NPCs/Base/HumanoidNPC`; shared changes go on the base. Create them with `Tools > LoomRoom > New Enemy` / `New NPC`; never put per-character data on managers. Layout: `Characters/{Scripts, Animations, Players/{Room,Table,Scripts,Input,Settings}, Enemies/{Base,Crypt,Town,_Archive}, NPCs/{Base,…}}` (retired enemies go to `Enemies/_Archive`, out of every biome list; the Green Slime was retired on 2026-10-15 and the fountain's "Something stirs" now lets out an Urn Crawler). Hit feel (knockback via `EnemyFX`, bone recoil via `HitReactionController`, the single hit flash in `CharacterFX`) lives on the enemy bases; the Humanoid enemy base also carries `EnemySwingTrail` and `HelmetPop`, and Crypt Rat carries its own `HitReactionController` (a non-humanoid rig tips as a whole). Crypt Rat, Cave Bat and Urn Crawler are the painted, rigged Meshy creatures (`Meshy/{Crypt_Rat_E,Cave_Bat_B,Urn_Crawler_B}`), set up once by a retired tool (removed 2026-10-13; it re-copied the clips over hand edits): takes copied into editable `.anim`s under `Model/`, the bite's `OnAttackHit` on its contact frame, the rat's controller with each one's walk and run speeds, world scale 1 so the paint keeps 150 texels per metre; the bat's material is two-sided for its wing membranes and it hovers by animation); the old box rat is in `Enemies/_Archive/Crypt Rat (box)`. Non-humanoid deaths keep playing the death clip while the body is thrown (`EnemyRagdoll.deathClipWhileThrown`), and `throwScale` shortens the throw for light bodies. Each creature's blood is `CharacterData.gore`. A name the game says as it is (no "the" or "a" in front, "Not a witch hits you") has `CharacterData.properName`; text builds names with `Character.TheName`/`TheNameCap`. A creature's hurt sound can be an AudioData (`CreatureAudio.painAudio`, plays instead of the Pain clips; the creature's pitch still applies): the skeletons share `Audio/Data/Skeleton grunt` (Grunt2 at half its old volume).
- **Generic effects + custom escape hatch.** `EffectEntry` is one serializable class with an enum `type` and Odin `[ShowIf]` per field; `Custom` routes to an `ItemEffect : ScriptableObject` (scripts in `Items/Scripts/Effects`). `InteractionEffect` / `InteractionAction` mirror this for interactables. Add new effect types by appending to the enum, adding a case in `ItemEffectProcessor.Apply`, and a `Describe` line.
- **Enums are serialized by integer.** Explicit values, append only, retired numbers stay retired (see `StatType`, `ItemType`, `EnemyState`).
- **Singletons never auto-create.** `Singleton<T>` returns null and logs if the manager is missing. Managers sit under the `Systems` object in the scene; most are prefab instances (`Assets/Game/Core/Prefabs` and elsewhere); `WorldManager` and `PlayerManager` are plain scene objects (still `Singleton<T>`); the Lighting Manager is not a singleton and is reached through `LightingManager.Instance`. Use `X.HasInstance` for cheap null-safe reads. Outside Play mode both `Instance` and `HasInstance` find the manager in the open scene (no log), so editor tools can use them.
- **Config lives on managers, containers live on players.** `InventoryManager` holds pickup and drop rules; `Inventory` (bag + hotbar, two components with different roles) and `Equipment` sit on each player so each keeps their own items and hands. The table player's bag is a grid (`Inventory.gridWidth` 10, 40 cells): an item takes `ItemData.GridSize` cells (`gridSize`, or 0 x 0 to pick by kind: swords and maces 1x3, knives 1x2, shields and spell tomes 2x2, body armour 2x3, helm, gloves, boots and leggings 2x2, the rest 1x1) and its stack sits at its top-left cell; new items go column by column (`FindSpot`); the hotbar stays plain slots (`gridWidth` 0). Hand items stay in the hotbar while held: a held item can't be dragged into the bag or dropped ("Put it away first") except by swapping places with another item for the same hand, which `Equipment` then takes in hand (it remembers each held item's hotbar slot); taking a held item off (a click on the doll, right-click or Unequip on its hotbar slot, `Equipment.TryUnequip`) moves it from its hotbar slot into the bag, or leaves it on the hotbar when the bag is full. Worn items (armour, leggings, trinkets) leave the bag while equipped and return on unequip, to the cell the replacement came from when it fits (`Equipment.UnequipInto` for a worn item dragged onto a bag cell).
- **One owner per concern.** `InputManager` owns the input asset (maps: `Room`, `Table`, `UI`, `Dev`); `GameManager` owns the game-state stack (`Explore`, `Menu`, `Dialogue`, `Cutscene`, `Dead`, `Inventory`, `Paused`) and therefore the cursor, which input map is live, and HUD visibility. Nothing else touches `Cursor` or enables action maps; the cursor is held to the state every focused frame (`GameManager.HoldCursor`, written only when it differs), so focus loss or Escape in the editor never leaves it showing while walking. States are reference-counted: every `Push` needs exactly one matching `Pop` from the same owner (`Dead` is a plain flag).
- **A swinging `DungeonDoor` moves its hinge to the opener's left and mirrors its leaf when the hinge is on the other side from where it was authored, so the handle is always on the far edge. Every hit is a `DamageInfo`.** Built by `Hitbox`, `EnemyBrain` or an effect; consumed by `CharacterStats.TakeDamage`; broadcast through `Character.Damaged` so FX, hit reactions, knockback and item triggers all see the same facts. `HitPoint` is where the blade met the body (`Hitbox` takes the point on the blade's line nearest the body) and `SwingDirection` is the blade's travel there; blood, gore and thrown kills follow it. `FactionRules.IsHostile` decides who can hurt whom. Armor takes a share of each physical hit: it comes off `CombatManager.armorEffectiveness` (0.65, so at most 65% of a hit is stopped) of a physical hit and the rest always lands; a successful block uses the whole hit. Each armour point stops `CombatManager.armorPerPoint` (0.2) damage: every armour number (items, enemies, `armorPerFloor`, `constitutionArmor`, `HelmetPop.armorLost`, the armour properties' `statPerPoint`) was multiplied by 5 on 2026-10-15 so rarity steps show as whole points (a Leather Helm is 4 since every armour item was lowered 20% on 2026-10-18); the shield bash counts armour x `armorPerPoint`.
- **Animator state is read by tag, never by name or layer index.** Arms windup/hold states are tagged `Attack`; the release states are `AttackRelease`, `AttackReleaseAlt` and `AttackHeavyRelease` (all four count as attacking). Arm block states are `Block` (only the LeftArm/RightArm layers; the FullBodyLayer state named "Block" is deliberately untagged). Enemy attack states are tagged `Attack`.
- **UI is authored in the scene.** Slots, the context menu, the drag ghost, notifications and vitals are scene objects; code only binds and fills them. Grandfathered: `ContextMenuUI` clones its authored button template; `DungeonHud` builds its canvas and minimap in code and `DungeonCombatFeedback` spawns enemy bars and damage numbers from the level's prefabs (moving them into the scene is open work).
- **Plain C# events.** UnityEvents only where a designer wires them (`InteractableTrigger.onInteract`, `CutsceneController.onStarted/onFinished`).
- **Save/load: `AdventureSave`** writes `loomroom-adventure.json` (a checkpoint at each floor, memorials, progression flags), atomically with a `.bak`. Items are saved by `ItemData.saveId`; never change one after release. Bump `AdventureSave.Data.Current` (4 since the bag grid) when the checkpoint shape changes (older checkpoints are then dropped, memorials and flags kept). `ItemStack`, `Inventory.slots`, `Equipment` and `ProgressionManager` flags stay plain (key, value) shapes; do not add non-serializable runtime state to them.
