# LoomRoom

First-person 3D RPG. Unity 6000.5, URP, Cinemachine 3, Input System, Odin Inspector, Pixel Crushers Dialogue System, Synty art. Two controllable players: the **Room player** (exploration, never fights) and the **Table player** (first-person arms, melee combat). Rewritten in September 2026 on the conventions used in LoomyDungeons.

## Tooling

- **Use the Unity CLI (`unity-cli` skill) for all editor, scene, prefab and asset work. Never use Unity MCP servers (coplay-mcp, UnityMCP), not even to check editor state.**
- **Meshy (AI models): `python Tools/meshy.py`** (text, image, retexture, remesh, rig, concept, fetch, balance; `--help` lists options). Results go to `Assets/Game/Meshy/<name>/` (FBX, GLB, textures, thumbnail, `meshy.json` with task ids for follow-ups); finished prefabs go in `Assets/Game/Meshy/Prefabs/` (for now), mesh, texture and material in `Prefabs/<Name>/`. Pixel-art props: `Tools/pixel_bake.py` (Blender: new UVs at wall density, bake) then a painter like `Tools/barrel_texture.py`; run Pixel Look > Build Materials after. The key is in `~/.meshy/api_key`, never in the repo. There is no Meshy MCP server.

- **Synty Pixel look (no Meshy)**: every Synty item, prop and room object is restyled like the rat: `Development/Editor/PixelRestyle.cs` (+ `.Apply.cs`) gives the model fresh UVs and paints a small point-filtered texture texel by texel: Synty's colour at that spot (so labels and sigils survive), baked ambient occlusion and dirt in creases, and material grain from 3D noise (wood, metal with rust, stone, bone, cloth, gold; pale modern surfaces in the room count as paint, no rust). Emissive parts keep a separate emission map. Results live in `Assets/Game/Art/Synty Pixel/{Weapons,Items,Props,Room}/<mesh>/` (mesh, texture, material); item and prop prefabs point at them, room objects carry them as prefab overrides in `Room.unity`; Synty assets are never edited (an item using a Synty prefab directly gets a copy in `Items/Prefabs`, like `Consumables/Potion`). Characters too (skinned meshes keep their bone weights): the DM body and every DM face, enemies and NPCs (on their base prefabs), the table player's arms (`TablePlayer.prefab`), the room player, the dinner family and the class miniatures (cast pewter; `ClassFigures` and `AdventureMemorial` no longer override their material). Skinned restyles live in `Characters/<mesh> (<source file>)/` because Synty reuses mesh names across files. The apartment blockout (`PixelRestyle.Surfaces.cs`) gets one 64 px tiling texture per flat-colour material (plaster, wood planks, linen, metal, stone) and box-projected UVs per shape and scale (`Art/Synty Pixel/Apartment/Meshes`); the room is built ~22 units to the metre, so `SurfaceTexelsPerMetre` is per unit (1.8). Textured materials (floorboards, paintings) get small point-filtered copies in `Surfaces/`. Glass, water, glows, text and the DM's eyes stay as they are. Review every rebuild against the Synty original before swapping it in. Textures are sized exactly from the unwrapped UV area (no power-of-two rounding), so every model gets the same pixel size.
- **Dirty pixel art (main art pipeline)**: the `loomroom-art` skill (`.claude/skills/loomroom-art/SKILL.md`) is the main art skill; the house style is in `Docs/Pixel-texture-style.md` (cave bat and urn crawler concepts). Creatures: Meshy concept in the house style, approval, then model. Weapons and shields are painted restyles (`Development/Editor/PixelRestyle.Painted.cs`: front and back reference renders repainted with `meshy.py concept`, projected back onto fresh UVs at `PixelRestyle.ItemTexelsPerUnit` 150), in `Art/Synty Pixel/{Weapons,Shields}/<mesh>/<Item> painted.*`; every weapon and shield prefab points at one. `PixelRestyle.Items.cs` (`Pixel Look > Restyle Weapons and Shields (dirty pixel)`, the noise-based Dirt grade, `<mesh> dirty.*`) was the first try and is not used. The Bronze Sword is `SM_Wep_Sword_03`.
## Settled decisions

- **Single `Assembly-CSharp`, no namespaces.** Same rule as LoomyDungeons. Do not propose asmdefs or namespaces.
- **One data asset per thing.** `CharacterData` holds what every character shares (identity, faction, stats, voice, animation triggers) and is used as-is by NPCs; `PlayerData : CharacterData` adds movement, stamina and starting items; `EnemyData : CharacterData` adds behaviour and attacks. Shared systems (health, damage, death) take `CharacterData`; player code reads `Player.PlayerData`, enemy AI reads `EnemyBrain.Data`. `ItemData` per item. A level's run is a list of `DungeonBiome` stages (look, enemies, loot profile, guardian); everyday loot is a `DungeonLootProfile` that picks catalog items by `ItemData.tier`, and hand-listed `DungeonLootTable`s are for special cases. Both are `LootSource`. `EnemyBehaviourProfile` only when several enemies must share tuning; otherwise behaviour lives on the enemy's own data. Everything lives under `Assets/Game/<Area>/`: all characters under `Characters/`, items in `Items/Data/{Weapons,Armor,Shields,Consumables,Trinkets}` (retired ones in `_Archive`), spells in `Combat/Spells/<Spell>`, custom effect assets in `Items/Effects`, audio data in `Audio/Data`, level assets in `Levels/`.
- **A character is one prefab file.** Its data (`PlayerData`, `EnemyData` or `CharacterData`) is stored inside the prefab as a sub-asset and edited on its Character component. Enemies are variants of `Characters/Enemies/Base/{Enemy base, Humanoid enemy base}`, NPCs of `Characters/NPCs/Base/HumanoidNPC`; shared changes go on the base. Create them with `Tools > LoomRoom > New Enemy` / `New NPC`; never put per-character data on managers. Layout: `Characters/{Scripts, Animations, Players/{Room,Table,Scripts,Input,Settings}, Enemies/{Base,Crypt,Town,_Archive}, NPCs/{Base,…}}` (retired enemies go to `Enemies/_Archive`, out of every biome list). Hit feel (knockback via `EnemyFX`, bone recoil via `HitReactionController`, the single hit flash in `CharacterFX`) lives on the enemy bases; the Humanoid enemy base also carries `EnemySwingTrail` and `HelmetPop`, and Crypt Rat and Green Slime carry their own `HitReactionController` (a non-humanoid rig tips as a whole). Each creature's blood is `CharacterData.gore`.
- **Generic effects + custom escape hatch.** `EffectEntry` is one serializable class with an enum `type` and Odin `[ShowIf]` per field; `Custom` routes to an `ItemEffect : ScriptableObject` (scripts in `Items/Scripts/Effects`). `InteractionEffect` / `InteractionAction` mirror this for interactables. Add new effect types by appending to the enum, adding a case in `ItemEffectProcessor.Apply`, and a `Describe` line.
- **Enums are serialized by integer.** Explicit values, append only, retired numbers stay retired (see `StatType`, `ItemType`, `EnemyState`).
- **Singletons never auto-create.** `Singleton<T>` returns null and logs if the manager is missing. Managers sit under the `Systems` object in the scene; most are prefab instances (`Assets/Game/Core/Prefabs` and elsewhere); `WorldManager` and `PlayerManager` are plain scene objects (still `Singleton<T>`); the Lighting Manager is not a singleton and is reached through `LightingManager.Instance`. Use `X.HasInstance` for cheap null-safe reads. Outside Play mode both `Instance` and `HasInstance` find the manager in the open scene (no log), so editor tools can use them.
- **Config lives on managers, containers live on players.** `InventoryManager` holds pickup and drop rules; `Inventory` (bag + hotbar, two components with different roles) and `Equipment` sit on each player so each keeps their own items and hands. The table player's bag is a Diablo grid (`Inventory.gridWidth` 10, 40 cells): an item takes `ItemData.GridSize` cells (`gridSize`, or 0 x 0 to pick by kind: swords and maces 1x3, knives 1x2, shields 2x2, body armour 2x3, helm, gloves, boots and leggings 2x2, the rest 1x1) and its stack sits at its top-left cell; new items go column by column (`FindSpot`); the hotbar stays plain slots (`gridWidth` 0). Hand items stay in the hotbar while held; worn items (armour, leggings, trinkets) leave the bag while equipped and return on unequip.
- **One owner per concern.** `InputManager` owns the input asset (maps: `Room`, `Table`, `UI`, `Dev`); `GameManager` owns the game-state stack (`Explore`, `Menu`, `Dialogue`, `Cutscene`, `Dead`, `Inventory`, `Paused`) and therefore the cursor, which input map is live, and HUD visibility. Nothing else touches `Cursor` or enables action maps. States are reference-counted: every `Push` needs exactly one matching `Pop` from the same owner (`Dead` is a plain flag).
- **Every hit is a `DamageInfo`.** Built by `Hitbox`, `EnemyBrain` or an effect; consumed by `CharacterStats.TakeDamage`; broadcast through `Character.Damaged` so FX, hit reactions, knockback and item triggers all see the same facts. `HitPoint` is where the blade met the body (`Hitbox` takes the point on the blade's line nearest the body) and `SwingDirection` is the blade's travel there; blood, gore and thrown kills follow it. `FactionRules.IsHostile` decides who can hurt whom. Armor works as in Barony: it comes off `CombatManager.armorEffectiveness` (0.75) of a physical hit and the rest always lands; a successful block uses the whole hit.
- **Animator state is read by tag, never by name or layer index.** Arms windup/hold states are tagged `Attack`; the release states are `AttackRelease`, `AttackReleaseAlt` and `AttackHeavyRelease` (all four count as attacking). Arm block states are `Block` (only the LeftArm/RightArm layers; the FullBodyLayer state named "Block" is deliberately untagged). Enemy attack states are tagged `Attack`.
- **UI is authored in the scene.** Slots, the context menu, the drag ghost, notifications and vitals are scene objects; code only binds and fills them. Grandfathered: `ContextMenuUI` clones its authored button template; `DungeonHud` builds its canvas and minimap in code and `DungeonCombatFeedback` spawns enemy bars and damage numbers from the level's prefabs (moving them into the scene is open work).
- **Plain C# events.** UnityEvents only where a designer wires them (`InteractableTrigger.onInteract`, `CutsceneController.onStarted/onFinished`).
- **Save/load: `AdventureSave`** writes `loomroom-adventure.json` (a checkpoint at each floor, memorials, progression flags), atomically with a `.bak`. Items are saved by `ItemData.saveId`; never change one after release. Bump `AdventureSave.Data.Current` (4 since the Diablo bag grid) when the checkpoint shape changes (older checkpoints are then dropped, memorials and flags kept). `ItemStack`, `Inventory.slots`, `Equipment` and `ProgressionManager` flags stay plain (key, value) shapes; do not add non-serializable runtime state to them.

## Where things are

All game code lives in `Assets/Game/<Area>/Scripts` (paths below are relative to `Assets/Game`). The only scene is `Scenes/Room.unity`.

| Concern | Files |
|---|---|
| Game state, input, players, world | `Core/Scripts/{GameManager,InputManager,PlayerManager,ProgressionManager,WorldManager,Singleton}.cs` |
| Screen effects and fades | `Core/Scripts/{ScreenManager,ScreenEffectSettings}.cs`, shader `Rendering/RetroScreen.shader` (full-screen pass on `Rendering/Desktop Renderer.asset`) |
| Pixel lighting | `ScreenManager` > Pixel lighting (on by default; F6 flips it in Play mode, Dev map): the game's own opaque materials switch in place between their own shader and `LoomRoom/Pixel Lit` (light per texel, stepped into bands), no camera filter. The materials and their original shaders are listed on the Pixel Look library (`lighting`); `Tools > LoomRoom > Pixel Look > Register Pixel Lighting Materials` (`Development/Editor/PixelLitConvert.cs`) adds new ones. Brightness per world is the library's `roomBrightness`/`tableBrightness`. **Pixel size** (same foldout, 1-8, any value, live) draws every texel that many times larger (`_PixelLookPixelSize`); the pixel textures have box mipmaps so larger pixels average their texels. **Texel size** (library > Pixel lighting > Texel size): pixels match across a world because each material is drawn with only as many texels across its texture (`_BaseTexels`) as its world's density gives where it is used: `tableTexelsPerUnit` 10.7 (the dungeon walls), `roomTexelsPerUnit` 1.8 (the apartment walls), characters x`characterDetail` and the table player's arms and all items x`firstPersonDetail` (14 each, so items and creatures painted at 150 texels per unit keep their texels); `keepTexels` lists surfaces that keep every texel (the big table's top, the DM's faces). Never sharper than the texture, at least 8 texels; such materials average only a quarter of each block (their textures are patchworks of UV islands). `Tools > LoomRoom > Pixel Look > Even Out Texel Sizes` measures every renderer in the scene and the game's prefabs and sets it; run it in Edit mode after new art or changed targets |
| Pixelator (3D pixel-art look) | `ScreenManager` > Pixelator (next to the full-screen "Retro pixel grid", which is a different thing): on/off, camera mode (Default, Clean, Low Res), and toggles for Architecture, Props and Characters; Play mode only, flips live. Texel scale is per world, not per player: renderers on rendering layer 8 (Room, tagged by `LightingManager.roomRoots`) use the library's room texel scale, everything else the table scale. `Core/Scripts/{PixelLook,PixelLookLibrary}.cs` swap each renderer between its original material and a `LoomRoom/Pixel Lit` copy (`Rendering/PixelLook/PixelLit.shader`: point-sampled colour, light evaluated per texel and stepped into bands, tiling patterns from `Rendering/PixelLook/Patterns`). Originals are never edited. `Tools > LoomRoom > Pixel Look > Build Materials` creates copies for new materials (keeps tuning); the library asset holds the pairs, their category, texel scales and each camera mode's effects. Low Res sets URP render scale and point upscaling through `ScreenManager` and restores them afterwards. Per-object pixelation (Object Pixel Size, Low-res Characters/Props) swaps to runtime twins on `Hidden/LoomRoom/Pixel Lit Object` (colour pass tagged `LoomPixelObject`), which `Core/Scripts/PixelObjectFeature.cs` (on the Desktop Renderer) draws at 1/N resolution and composites back with depth (the buffer's depth is first seeded with the scene's depth prepass, farthest per block, so geometry hidden behind the scene, such as the room under the dungeon, never bleeds out as an edge; with the seed the composite skips its own depth test, which would stripe flat props); transparent originals become a screen-door dither (`_DitherAlpha`). `PixelLit.shader` and `PixelLitObject.shader` share `PixelLitForward.hlsl` and must keep identical properties |
| Lighting and moods | `World/LightingManager.cs` + `LightingManager.Moods.cs`, presets are `SceneMood` assets (`Core/Scripts/SceneMood.cs`) |
| Character layer | `Characters/Scripts/{Character,CharacterStats,CharacterData,PlayerData,EnemyData,CreatureAudio,Faction,StatType,StatModifier,CharacterFX,GoreProfile,HelmetPop}.cs`, `Characters/Scripts/Editor/NewCharacterWindow.cs`. `CharacterData.gore` (`GoreProfile`, Presentation > Gore): `BloodKind` (None, Blood, Slime, BoneDust), colour, amount, `burstOnDeath` (slimes), kill sound |
| Enemy / NPC AI | `Characters/Scripts/AI/{EnemyBrain,EnemyAttackRunner,Perception,EnemyMotor,EnemyAttack,EnemyArchery,EnemyBehaviourProfile,NpcBrain,NoiseEvents,EnemySwingTrail}.cs`. An enemy's whoosh plays just before its blow lands (`EnemyAttackRunner.HitAt`, read from the clip's hit event; `EnemyBrain.SwingHitAt`), and `EnemySwingTrail` draws a short pale streak on its weapon around that moment. `EnemyData.archer` makes any enemy an archer: the bow shot joins its attacks, the bow sits in the left hand (`EnemyWeaponLoadout`, grips in `Characters/Resources`), and the release clip's `OnAttackHit` looses a `Combat/Arrows/Arrow` (`ArrowProjectile`) |
| Player | `Characters/Players/Scripts/{Player,PlayerMotor,PlayerLook,PlayerFootsteps,PlayerCombat,PlayerCameraRig,PlayerAnimation,PlayerSettings}.cs`, MFPC modules in `Characters/Players/Scripts/Modules`. The Table camera is 85 degrees; the arms and held items are drawn as through `PlayerViewPresentation.viewModelFieldOfView` (70) by scaling `FirstPersonVisuals` sideways and up around the camera. `PlayerInput.cs` is generated from `Characters/Players/Input/PlayerInput.inputactions`; never hand-edit it |
| Combat | `Combat/Scripts/{CombatManager,Hitbox,WeaponAnimationRelay,IDamageable,GoreManager,GoreDroplets,SlashLines}.cs`; values and defaults in `Docs/CombatFeel.md`, plan in `Docs/Heathen-rework-steps.md`. Pace is Heathen's: a click strikes straight from the windup (`FirstPersonTable.controller` transitions), but a new light swing can't start sooner than `CombatManager.swingInterval` (0.6 s, divided by the AttackSpeed stat) after the last one began. No hit stop (`hitStopEnabled`, `hitStopOnPlayerHurt` off). `attackFromBlock`: clicking with the shield up lowers it for the swing and raises it again if block is still held. Light swings are the original clips (`simpleSwings` on); the Heathen swing set (see Table player arms animation) was rejected and stays off. `weaponSweepSteps` 12; the player's melee line of sight starts at the camera, so the floor doesn't block a stab at a rat. `CombatManager.personalSpace` (0.75) keeps players and enemies apart (`PlayerMotor` blocks walking in, `EnemyBrain` steps back). Enemies can't be stunlocked: a light hit flinches an enemy for `lightFlinchDuration` 0.25 s but never mid-swing; `hitsInterruptWindup` (off) lets a hit within `interruptWindow` (0.45 s) of a windup cancel that attack, and the attack after an interrupt can't be interrupted; staggers have a 1.5 s immunity; knockback is `enemyKnockbackScale` 1.4. Getting hit: a deep red screen held `hurtScreenHold` 0.1 s, then faded; the camera jolts away from the hit (`PlayerFX`). Kills are thrown: `EnemyRagdoll` sets every body part's velocity (`ragdollImpulse` 5.5 m/s along the blow and the swing, x1.4 for heavy hits and criticals, `ragdollUpwardImpulse` 3), and bodies pass through living enemies (`DeathPhysics.IgnorePlayers`). Impact (`Tools > LoomRoom > Combat > Set Up Heathen Impact`, `Combat/Scripts/Editor/HeathenCombatSetup.cs`, safe to run again): a contact flash replaces the dust puff (`hitParticlePrefabs`), `SlashLines` on the table player's hitbox draws thin streaks along the arc, `HelmetPop` knocks a helmet off on 30% of head hits (the enemy loses 1 armour; the helmet stays on the floor), and `GoreManager` (prefab `Combat/Prefabs/GoreManager` under Systems, art in `Combat/Effects/Gore`) sprays the victim's `CharacterData.gore` from the hit point along the swing; drops that land (`GoreDroplets`) leave stains for the floor (cap 260), skeletons spill bone dust and chips with no stains, `burstOnDeath` bodies burst into a spray and chunks, and each profile has a kill sound (`Audio/Data/Kill {bones,flesh,slime}`). `UI/Scripts/CrosshairUI.cs`: a dot while the table player fights, a shield icon while blocking (both authored in the scene). Damage numbers appear at the hit point (white, `PIXEARG_`), criticals and backstabs as "CRITICAL!" or "BACKSTAB!" above a bigger number that grows in; the centre-screen result line is off (`DungeonCombatFeedback.resultLine`). Skeletons burst into their own bones on death (`Characters/Scripts/DeathBurst.cs`, per enemy: the skinned mesh is baked and split per humanoid bone; enemies without it fall back to `EnemyRagdoll` on the Humanoid enemy base; shared death code in `DeathPhysics`). The enemy last hit or aimed at shows one bar with its name at the top of the screen (`DungeonCombatFeedback.topBar`) |
