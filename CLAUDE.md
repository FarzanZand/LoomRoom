# LoomRoom

The game is called **The Table Waits** (the product name since 2026-10-13); the project, repository and code keep the name LoomRoom. First-person 3D RPG. Unity 6000.5, URP, Cinemachine 3, Input System, Odin Inspector, Pixel Crushers Dialogue System, Synty art. Two controllable players: the **Room player** (exploration, never fights) and the **Table player** (first-person arms, melee combat). Rewritten in September 2026 on the conventions used in LoomyDungeons.

## Tooling

- **Use the Unity CLI (`unity-cli` skill) for all editor, scene, prefab and asset work. Never use Unity MCP servers (coplay-mcp, UnityMCP), not even to check editor state.**
- **Meshy (AI models): `python Tools/meshy.py`** (text, image, retexture, remesh, rig, concept, fetch, balance; `--help` lists options). Results go to `Assets/Game/Meshy/<name>/` (FBX, GLB, textures, thumbnail, `meshy.json` with task ids for follow-ups); finished prefabs go in `Assets/Game/Meshy/Prefabs/` (for now), mesh, texture and material in `Prefabs/<Name>/`. Pixel-art props: `Tools/pixel_bake.py` (Blender: new UVs at wall density, bake) then a painter like `Tools/barrel_texture.py`; run Pixel Look > Build Materials after. The key is in `~/.meshy/api_key`, never in the repo. There is no Meshy MCP server.

- **Synty Pixel look (no Meshy)**: every Synty item, prop and room object is restyled like the rat: `Development/Editor/PixelRestyle.cs` (+ `.Apply.cs`) gives the model fresh UVs and paints a small point-filtered texture texel by texel: Synty's colour at that spot (so labels and sigils survive), baked ambient occlusion and dirt in creases, and material grain from 3D noise (wood, metal with rust, stone, bone, cloth, gold; pale modern surfaces in the room count as paint, no rust). Emissive parts keep a separate emission map. Results live in `Assets/Game/Art/Synty Pixel/{Weapons,Items,Props,Room}/<mesh>/` (mesh, texture, material); item and prop prefabs point at them, room objects carry them as prefab overrides in `Room.unity`; Synty assets are never edited (an item using a Synty prefab directly gets a copy in `Items/Prefabs`, like `Consumables/Potion`). Characters too (skinned meshes keep their bone weights): the DM body and every DM face, enemies and NPCs (on their base prefabs), the table player's arms (`TablePlayer.prefab`), the room player, the dinner family and the class miniatures (cast pewter; `ClassFigures` and `AdventureMemorial` no longer override their material). Skinned restyles live in `Characters/<mesh> (<source file>)/` because Synty reuses mesh names across files. The apartment blockout (`PixelRestyle.Surfaces.cs`) gets one 64 px tiling texture per flat-colour material (plaster, wood planks, linen, metal, stone) and box-projected UVs per shape and scale (`Art/Synty Pixel/Apartment/Meshes`); the room is built ~22 units to the metre, so `SurfaceTexelsPerMetre` is per unit (1.8). Textured materials (floorboards, paintings) get small point-filtered copies in `Surfaces/`. Glass, water, glows, text and the DM's eyes stay as they are. Review every rebuild against the Synty original before swapping it in. Textures are sized exactly from the unwrapped UV area (no power-of-two rounding), so every model gets the same pixel size. Restyled meshes are saved readable (`PixelRestyle.MarkReadable`): prop mesh colliders use them, and the NavMesh built with each floor can only read readable meshes in a build (never point a collider at a Synty mesh).
- **Dirty pixel art (main art pipeline)**: the `loomroom-art` skill (`.claude/skills/loomroom-art/SKILL.md`) is the main art skill; the house style is in `Docs/Pixel-texture-style.md` (cave bat and urn crawler concepts). Creatures: Meshy concept in the house style, approval, then model. Weapons and shields are painted restyles (`Development/Editor/PixelRestyle.Painted.cs`: front and back reference renders repainted with `meshy.py concept`, projected back onto fresh UVs at `PixelRestyle.ItemTexelsPerUnit`, 128 texels per unit for all new art since 2026-10-12; older art is at 150 until re-baked), in `Art/Synty Pixel/{Weapons,Shields}/<mesh>/<Item> painted.*`; every weapon and shield prefab points at one. The first try (a noise-based "dirty" grade, `<mesh> dirty.*` files) is retired; `PixelRestyle.Items.cs` now only holds `ItemTexelsPerUnit`. The Bronze Sword is `SM_Wep_Sword_03`.
## Settled decisions

- **Our own design.** Other games were studied for feel and are inspiration only: never name them in code, assets, docs, commit messages or the game, and make our version of anything taken from them our own, not a copy.
- **Single `Assembly-CSharp`, no namespaces.** Same rule as LoomyDungeons. Do not propose asmdefs or namespaces.
- **One data asset per thing.** `CharacterData` holds what every character shares (identity, faction, stats, voice, animation triggers) and is used as-is by NPCs; `PlayerData : CharacterData` adds movement, stamina and starting items; `EnemyData : CharacterData` adds behaviour and attacks. Shared systems (health, damage, death) take `CharacterData`; player code reads `Player.PlayerData`, enemy AI reads `EnemyBrain.Data`. `ItemData` per item. A level's run is a list of `DungeonBiome` stages (look, enemies, loot profile, guardian); everyday loot is a `DungeonLootProfile` that picks catalog items by `ItemData.tier`, and hand-listed `DungeonLootTable`s are for special cases. Both are `LootSource`. `EnemyBehaviourProfile` only when several enemies must share tuning; otherwise behaviour lives on the enemy's own data. Everything lives under `Assets/Game/<Area>/`: all characters under `Characters/`, items in `Items/Data/{Weapons,Armor,Shields,Consumables,Trinkets}` (retired ones in `_Archive`), spells in `Combat/Spells/<Spell>`, custom effect assets in `Items/Effects`, audio data in `Audio/Data`, level assets in `Levels/`.
- **A character is one prefab file.** Its data (`PlayerData`, `EnemyData` or `CharacterData`) is stored inside the prefab as a sub-asset and edited on its Character component. Enemies are variants of `Characters/Enemies/Base/{Enemy base, Humanoid enemy base}`, NPCs of `Characters/NPCs/Base/HumanoidNPC`; shared changes go on the base. Create them with `Tools > LoomRoom > New Enemy` / `New NPC`; never put per-character data on managers. Layout: `Characters/{Scripts, Animations, Players/{Room,Table,Scripts,Input,Settings}, Enemies/{Base,Crypt,Town,_Archive}, NPCs/{Base,…}}` (retired enemies go to `Enemies/_Archive`, out of every biome list). Hit feel (knockback via `EnemyFX`, bone recoil via `HitReactionController`, the single hit flash in `CharacterFX`) lives on the enemy bases; the Humanoid enemy base also carries `EnemySwingTrail` and `HelmetPop`, and Crypt Rat and Green Slime carry their own `HitReactionController` (a non-humanoid rig tips as a whole). Crypt Rat, Cave Bat and Urn Crawler are the painted, rigged Meshy creatures (`Meshy/{Crypt_Rat_E,Cave_Bat_B,Urn_Crawler_B}`), set up once by a retired tool (removed 2026-10-13; it re-copied the clips over hand edits): takes copied into editable `.anim`s under `Model/`, the bite's `OnAttackHit` on its contact frame, the rat's controller with each one's walk and run speeds, world scale 1 so the paint keeps 150 texels per metre; the bat's material is two-sided for its wing membranes and it hovers by animation); the old box rat is in `Enemies/_Archive/Crypt Rat (box)`. Non-humanoid deaths keep playing the death clip while the body is thrown (`EnemyRagdoll.deathClipWhileThrown`), and `throwScale` shortens the throw for light bodies. Each creature's blood is `CharacterData.gore`.
- **Generic effects + custom escape hatch.** `EffectEntry` is one serializable class with an enum `type` and Odin `[ShowIf]` per field; `Custom` routes to an `ItemEffect : ScriptableObject` (scripts in `Items/Scripts/Effects`). `InteractionEffect` / `InteractionAction` mirror this for interactables. Add new effect types by appending to the enum, adding a case in `ItemEffectProcessor.Apply`, and a `Describe` line.
- **Enums are serialized by integer.** Explicit values, append only, retired numbers stay retired (see `StatType`, `ItemType`, `EnemyState`).
- **Singletons never auto-create.** `Singleton<T>` returns null and logs if the manager is missing. Managers sit under the `Systems` object in the scene; most are prefab instances (`Assets/Game/Core/Prefabs` and elsewhere); `WorldManager` and `PlayerManager` are plain scene objects (still `Singleton<T>`); the Lighting Manager is not a singleton and is reached through `LightingManager.Instance`. Use `X.HasInstance` for cheap null-safe reads. Outside Play mode both `Instance` and `HasInstance` find the manager in the open scene (no log), so editor tools can use them.
- **Config lives on managers, containers live on players.** `InventoryManager` holds pickup and drop rules; `Inventory` (bag + hotbar, two components with different roles) and `Equipment` sit on each player so each keeps their own items and hands. The table player's bag is a Diablo grid (`Inventory.gridWidth` 10, 40 cells): an item takes `ItemData.GridSize` cells (`gridSize`, or 0 x 0 to pick by kind: swords and maces 1x3, knives 1x2, shields 2x2, body armour 2x3, helm, gloves, boots and leggings 2x2, the rest 1x1) and its stack sits at its top-left cell; new items go column by column (`FindSpot`); the hotbar stays plain slots (`gridWidth` 0). Hand items stay in the hotbar while held: a held item can't be dragged into the bag or dropped ("Put it away first") except by swapping places with another item for the same hand, which `Equipment` then takes in hand (it remembers each held item's hotbar slot); taking a held item off (a click on the doll, right-click or Unequip on its hotbar slot, `Equipment.TryUnequip`) moves it from its hotbar slot into the bag, or leaves it on the hotbar when the bag is full. Worn items (armour, leggings, trinkets) leave the bag while equipped and return on unequip, to the cell the replacement came from when it fits (`Equipment.UnequipInto` for a worn item dragged onto a bag cell).
- **One owner per concern.** `InputManager` owns the input asset (maps: `Room`, `Table`, `UI`, `Dev`); `GameManager` owns the game-state stack (`Explore`, `Menu`, `Dialogue`, `Cutscene`, `Dead`, `Inventory`, `Paused`) and therefore the cursor, which input map is live, and HUD visibility. Nothing else touches `Cursor` or enables action maps. States are reference-counted: every `Push` needs exactly one matching `Pop` from the same owner (`Dead` is a plain flag).
- **Every hit is a `DamageInfo`.** Built by `Hitbox`, `EnemyBrain` or an effect; consumed by `CharacterStats.TakeDamage`; broadcast through `Character.Damaged` so FX, hit reactions, knockback and item triggers all see the same facts. `HitPoint` is where the blade met the body (`Hitbox` takes the point on the blade's line nearest the body) and `SwingDirection` is the blade's travel there; blood, gore and thrown kills follow it. `FactionRules.IsHostile` decides who can hurt whom. Armor works as in Barony: it comes off `CombatManager.armorEffectiveness` (0.75) of a physical hit and the rest always lands; a successful block uses the whole hit.
- **Animator state is read by tag, never by name or layer index.** Arms windup/hold states are tagged `Attack`; the release states are `AttackRelease`, `AttackReleaseAlt` and `AttackHeavyRelease` (all four count as attacking). Arm block states are `Block` (only the LeftArm/RightArm layers; the FullBodyLayer state named "Block" is deliberately untagged). Enemy attack states are tagged `Attack`.
- **UI is authored in the scene.** Slots, the context menu, the drag ghost, notifications and vitals are scene objects; code only binds and fills them. Grandfathered: `ContextMenuUI` clones its authored button template; `DungeonHud` builds its canvas and minimap in code and `DungeonCombatFeedback` spawns enemy bars and damage numbers from the level's prefabs (moving them into the scene is open work).
- **Plain C# events.** UnityEvents only where a designer wires them (`InteractableTrigger.onInteract`, `CutsceneController.onStarted/onFinished`).
- **Save/load: `AdventureSave`** writes `loomroom-adventure.json` (a checkpoint at each floor, memorials, progression flags), atomically with a `.bak`. Items are saved by `ItemData.saveId`; never change one after release. Bump `AdventureSave.Data.Current` (4 since the Diablo bag grid) when the checkpoint shape changes (older checkpoints are then dropped, memorials and flags kept). `ItemStack`, `Inventory.slots`, `Equipment` and `ProgressionManager` flags stay plain (key, value) shapes; do not add non-serializable runtime state to them.
