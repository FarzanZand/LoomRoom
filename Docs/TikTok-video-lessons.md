# TikTok game video lessons

Running notes from making LoomRoom TikToks (October 2026). Kept so a future video tool can follow them for any game.

## What the viewer responded to
- **Mystery beats spectacle.** The best opening was the game's own: darkness, two glowing eyes, a voice ("There you are."), a lamp lighting a table. Keep it intact; do not cut away from it early.
- **The game's own dialogue as on-screen text** works far better than invented captions. Quote it exactly, in the game's dialogue colour.
- **One soundtrack, low, under the game's sound.** Never lay music over the game's own music: record with game music muted.
- **Don't show what isn't polished.** Ugly or unfinished models (here: the DM's face, the boss) hurt more than they help. Ask what the developer is proud of.

## Footage
- **Record clean frames:** hide the HUD, the game's subtitles and prompts while recording. Covering them later with dark bars looks cheap.
- **Verify the camera tooling first.** A sign error in look pitch made every scripted fight stare at the ceiling and fire spells into the air. Test-record one short shot and look at it before recording a session.
- **Stage combat, don't simulate it.** Bring one enemy at a time to 5–7 m in front of the camera, aim at its chest, cast, then finish it in melee. Turn to clear floor before each new enemy so corpses don't block the view. Log whether each spell hit and only use hits.
- **Native framing matters.** Portrait recording is great in tight spaces (intro, dungeon corridors) but crops out wide set pieces (character sheet, table). Record wide set pieces in 4K landscape and crop in the edit.

## Editing
- **Review every render frame by frame before calling it done** (contact sheets at 0.25–0.5 s, plus single full-size frames for text). Check: framing, readable text, nothing blocking the subject, no clashing UI.
- Cut slower in the quiet, dark parts; soft fades, not hard cuts, in darkness.
- Text: large pixel font, upper third, inside TikTok's safe area, one line of dialogue per shot.
- End card: the game's own image (the eyes in the dark), not a blurred smear.
- Loudness about -14 LUFS, peaks under -1 dB; 1080x1920, 30 fps, H.264, AAC.

## Round 4 additions
- **Never show the same thing twice in one video** (two dice rolls, two builds of the same level, the same location twice).
- **Start before the first word.** Find each line's onset from the voice audio and cut in the pauses between lines; never open mid-sentence. Fade in from black at the very start.
- **Show set pieces whole.** The tutorial board build lasts about a second in real time: record at higher resolution, slow it in the edit (0.45x) and zoom in on it, trimmed to the build itself (no static tail).
- **Frame for the subject, not the format.** When a scene is wider than 9:16 (dice and character sheet), show the region across the width over a blurred copy instead of cropping half of it away.
- **Footage can reveal game bugs.** Skeleton bursts snapped to another pose and hopped (scaled mesh bake, Animator still running on the death frame); fixed in the game, not hidden in the edit.
- **Watch the tails of shots:** camera pans at the end of a take can reveal things that should stay off screen (here, the DM's face).
